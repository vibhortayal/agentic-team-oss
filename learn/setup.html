Set up GitHub for your team

This happens after you say yes to the plan in your first project. Part 1 is all you need for a first try. Part 2 makes the checks strict, for an app real people will use. You don't need to find your way around GitHub: Ella changes its settings for you, using your sign-in, after your yes. You only click where GitHub needs a real person.

Part 1 · For a first try

You need these. About 20 minutes, most of it waiting for Ella.

1

Check what Ella already did

During setup, Ella signed your computer in to GitHub. You saw a short code, your browser opened, and you clicked Authorize. She also made your app's repository: its private folder on GitHub, where the team saves its work. Only you can see it.

Skipped any of that? Ask Ella "help me connect to GitHub". She picks up where setup stopped.

usually done already, once per computer
2

Say yes to the plan

This is the plan from your first project. When you say yes, the team gets to work in your repository.

3

Add the automatic checks

Otto, the team's ops agent, adds the automatic checks to your project. They run on GitHub every time something changes, and catch any agent that skips a step.

Ella tells you in plain words what they do and asks for your OK. Say yes, and they're added. You don't need to read any code.

about 2 minutes
4

Settings and keys

The checks need to know a few things about your project. Ella fills them in for you after you say yes. You don't type anything, and you never open GitHub's settings.

TEAM_OWNERYour GitHub name, so the checks know whose OK counts.
LINT_CMDHow the checks look at code style. On a first try it's off.
SETUP_CMDHow the checks get your app ready to test.

Later your app may need a key, like a password for a service it uses. A key is secret, so never paste it into a chat, not even with Ella. Ella sends you a link to where you make the key. Then open your progress board, press Settings and keys, paste it in the box and press Save. It goes straight to GitHub, locked, and nobody can read it back.

No board yet? Ella gives you one line to paste instead. On a Mac, open Terminal. On Windows, open PowerShell. Paste the line, press Enter, then paste your key. You won't see it as you paste. That's normal.

about 1 minute

That's it. The team can start building.

One honest note. On a first try, the agents use your own GitHub sign-in. So the checks can't tell your OK from an agent's. They still run and show problems, but they can't stop anything on their own. So before you say yes to a new version, try it yourself in the preview Ella sends, like a tester would. That's fine for a test app. Before real people use your app, do Part 2.

Part 2 · Before real people use your app

Skip these on a first try. They make the checks strict, so no agent can skip a step. About 30 minutes.

5

A second GitHub account for the builders

The building agents need their own account, so GitHub can tell their work from yours. GitHub allows one free extra account like this per person, for this kind of job.

Only a person can make a GitHub account, so this part is yours. Ella opens each page and tells you what to click.

  1. In a private browser window, make a new GitHub account. Use a name like yourname-agents. Turn on two-step sign-in.
  2. Tell Ella the new name. She invites it to your app's repository. Open the invite email in the new account and click Accept.
  3. Still in the new account, Ella opens the page where it makes a key, and tells you which boxes to tick. Click Generate.
  4. Save the key in your safe place for keys. Never paste it into a chat.
about 10 minutes
6

A checker app for Vera

Vera checks the work. She gets her own GitHub app, so her approval can't be faked by the agents who built it.

  1. Ella opens GitHub's page for a new app with everything already filled in. Check the name and click Create.
  2. Click "Generate a private key". A key file downloads. Put it in your safe place for keys.
  3. Click "Install App", then pick your app's repository.

Ella looks up the app's name and ID numbers herself and adds them where they're needed. You only keep the key file.

about 10 minutes
7

Keep the two keys apart

The builders' key and Vera's key must never be in the same place. If they were, a builder could approve its own work.

Most coding assistants have a place for secret settings, such as separate workspaces or environments. Put the builders' key in one, and Vera's app ID and key file in another that only Vera's sessions use. Ella tells you where this is in your coding assistant. If yours has no way to keep them apart, you start Vera's sessions yourself.

about 10 minutes
8

Turn on the branch rules

These rules make GitHub refuse any change that hasn't passed the checks and your review. Before you start, Ella checks your GitHub plan. On a free plan, GitHub may only enforce these rules on public repositories. If you need a paid plan, she tells you the price first.

Ella tells you in plain words what the rules do. In short: every change must pass the checks and get your yes, and nobody but you can skip them. Say yes, and she turns them on.

Ella does this. You just say yes.
9

Watch a practice run

Otto tests the checks before real work starts. He sends six practice changes that should all be stopped, such as one that edits a test. Then he sends one good change that should pass. Vera writes down what happened, and Ella tells you in one line if it all worked.

Otto does this. You don't need to do anything.
