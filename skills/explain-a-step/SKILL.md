# Explain a step

The person uses phones, apps and chat assistants, but hasn't used GitHub or built software. They want to get the step done and understand roughly why, not take a course. One step at a time.

## Shape

1. **What it is,** in one everyday sentence. A comparison helps when it's accurate: "A pull request is a proposed change waiting for your OK, like a tracked-changes document."
2. **Why now,** in one sentence: what it unlocks, or what goes wrong without it.
3. **What to do,** as numbered steps, five at most, with the exact button and page names they'll see. If it takes more than five, split it and do the first part now.
4. **How long and what it costs.** "About 5 minutes, free." If it costs money, give the monthly figure and wait for their OK.
5. **One good link** for anyone who wants more: the service's own help page first, a well-known beginner video second. Open it before sharing it, and drop it if it's out of date.
6. **Check back:** ask them to tell you when it's done, or what they see if it looks different.

Write it so a 10-year-old could follow it, without talking down: sentences of 15 words or fewer, everyday words, and any technical word explained the first time.

Then stop. Don't add the next three steps or the history of the tool.

## Rules

- **Plain words.** If a technical word is unavoidable, explain it the first time. The [short glossary](references/plain-words.md) has wording for the common ones.
- **Secrets never go in the chat.** When a step involves a password, key or token, tell them exactly where to put it (the service's settings page, the project's secret store) and never ask them to paste it to you.
- **Say when you're not sure** the screens look the same for them ("this may have moved; look for ..."). Don't guess silently.
- **Match their pace.** Short by default; give more detail only when they ask. If they push back on a step that matters, explain what it protects and offer a safer workaround rather than dropping it.
- **Do it for them when you can.** Explain only the steps that need their hands: their accounts, their approvals, their money.
